= Cite an FPSOT spec in a tender
:page-type: how-to
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This guide takes a public buyer from the choice of a specification to the scoring of bids.
It uses the draft clause modules of the {abbr-fpsot-first}.
Each step cites the rule it relies on.
The modules are drafts and are not legal advice, so your own counsel must review your tender.

== Before you start

Check whether {abbr-fpsot} has published a {term-clause-pack} for the specification that you need, under PRC-040.
A spec-specific pack exists only when an implementation that {abbr-fpsot} did not make passes the suite.
Two independent integrators that have deployed the open building block in production can also meet this condition.
Without one, only the generic modules exist, and you take on more legal risk.
Read the page "Questions for counsel" of this specification, and give it to your lawyer.

Find your legal regime, under PRC-015:

* a Québec public body, such as a ministry or a school or health body, uses the {abbr-rcti-first} variant;
* a Québec municipal body uses the {abbr-lcom-first} variant;
* a federal department uses the federal variant.

In Québec, use the French text of each module and of the specification, under PRC-005.

== Choose the specification and its classes

Pick the exact version of the specification, under PRC-020.
Pick the {term-conformance-class}es that your need requires.
From version 1.0.0, decide whether you accept a later minor version of the same major version.
Below version 1.0.0, accept only later patch releases.
Write down the functional need behind your choice, such as interoperability or a clean exit, under PRC-023.

== Paste the modules

Copy module M01 and module M02 from `clauses/en/` or `clauses/fr/`, or open their Word or OpenDocument files.
Fill in each field in square brackets, under PRC-013.
Keep the words "or equivalent" each time the text names the specification or the suite, under PRC-022.
Never add the name of a product, including software from {abbr-fpsot}, under PRC-021.

A clause of module M01, with its fields still open, could read as follows.

[source,text]
----
The solution must conform to the FPSOT [name] specification,
version [1.0], conformance classes [server] and [client], or equivalent.
The bidder does not have to use FPSOT software.
----

Add the other modules that your need calls for: license, repository, data and exit, accessibility, language and security.
Each module states its own fields and choices in its drafting note.

== Set the award and the costs

Use module M08.
Make each required conformance class a pass or fail condition, under PRC-038.
Keep or change the default weights, and make sure they total 100.
Fill in the cost-element worksheet before you publish the tender, under PRC-039.

Under the {abbr-rcti}, the tender documents must name these elements in advance.
Under the {abbr-lcom}, use the worksheet only to assess the need, since prices cannot yet carry additional costs.
Check the table of trade-agreement thresholds against the values in force on the date of your tender, under PRC-018.

== Clean up and review

Remove each drafting note.
Keep the credit line of each module, under PRC-006.
Remove the draft banner only after your counsel approves the text.
Send any open question to the public channels of {abbr-fpsot}, under PRC-052, since it gives no private help.

== Evaluate the bids

Check that each bid holds a {term-conformance-statement} and a suite report, or an equivalent test report, under PRC-024.
Treat the statement as pass or fail, and the report as the proof.
Assess each claim of equivalence on the same footing as the other bids, under PRC-022.
You may compare a bid with the public register of results, but you must not require a listing, under PRC-046.
After the award, run the same suite in your acceptance tests, under PRC-025.
