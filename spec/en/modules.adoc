= Clause modules
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

A {term-clause-module} is one block of clause text that a buyer pastes into a tender as a whole.
These rules set the parts and the versions of each module.
They also set its variants by legal regime, and the sources that its text may draw on.

== Form of a module

[[PRC-010]]
*PRC-010.* A {term-clause-pack} MUST hold the nine modules of the table on the xref:index.adoc[first page], M01 to M09.
Each module MUST apply to any {abbr-fpsot-first} specification, unless its title marks it as specific to one.
A spec-specific module MUST name the specification, its version and the conformance classes it covers.
A generic module leaves the specification as a field that the buyer fills in.
A small, fixed set of modules lets buyers learn the kit once.

[[PRC-011]]
*PRC-011.* Each module MUST have these parts, in this order:

* the draft banner of PRC-012, when it applies;
* the copyright and license line of PRC-007;
* a header with the module ID, title, version, date and status;
* a drafting note for the buyer, which the buyer removes before it publishes the tender;
* the clause text, in one or more variants;
* the list of fields of PRC-013;
* the credit line of PRC-006.

The drafting note MUST give the purpose of the clause, its functional need and the choices of the buyer.
It MUST also give the statements of PRC-002 and PRC-009.
A fixed order lets a buyer find each part, and lets a test check that each part exists.

[[PRC-012]]
*PRC-012.* Each module MUST start with a draft banner until a lawyer reviews it and the pack records that review.
In English the banner MUST read `Draft — do not use in a tender before legal review`.
In French it MUST read `Ébauche — ne pas utiliser dans un appel d'offres avant l'avis d'un conseiller juridique`.
A spec-specific module MUST also keep the banner while its specification does not meet PRC-040.
A clause that nobody has checked against the law can harm the buyer that uses it.
The banner makes this risk plain on the first line.

[[PRC-013]]
*PRC-013.* Each field that the buyer fills in MUST appear in square brackets, such as `[specification name]`.
The list of fields MUST explain each one, and MUST give any suggested value.
The clause text MUST NOT hold a binding value that the list of fields does not name.
Clear fields stop a buyer from publishing a placeholder, or a default that it did not choose.

[[PRC-014]]
*PRC-014.* Each module MUST have its own version number under link:https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html[Semantic Versioning 2.0.0], separate from the pack.
A change to what the bidder must do MUST raise the major number.
A new option or variant MUST raise the minor number.
A fix of wording that keeps the meaning raises the patch number.
The module MUST list its changes in its own history.
A buyer that pasted version 1.2.0 can then see at once whether version 1.3.0 changes anything it relies on.

== Variants by legal regime

[[PRC-015]]
*PRC-015.* A module MUST give separate variants where the law differs between these regimes:

* the public bodies of Québec, which buy information technology under the {abbr-lcop-first} and the {abbr-rcti-first};
* the municipal bodies of Québec, which buy under the {abbr-lcom-first} since 2026-04-01;
* the federal government.

The heading of each variant MUST name its regime.
A module with one text for all regimes MUST say so in its drafting note.
Two regimes in Québec set different rules on the same points.
For example, the {abbr-rcti} lets a provincial body add costs to the bid prices, for a contract that includes goods.
A municipal body cannot yet do so, says the link:https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/affaires-municipales/publications/muni_express/GUI_muni_express_lcom.pdf[guide on the act] of the ministry.

[[PRC-016]]
*PRC-016.* A module MUST NOT state an article number as a fact unless a lawyer has confirmed it.
Until then, it MUST mark the number `to be verified`, in French `à vérifier`.
It MUST also name the text it read and its date, such as the date of a consolidated text.
The research of {abbr-fpsot} could not read the official legal database of Québec.
The reforms of 2025 and 2026 also changed several texts.
A wrong number in a tender misleads every bidder that reads it.

[[PRC-017]]
*PRC-017.* A module MUST fit into the tender documents of the buyer, as an annex or a section.
It MUST NOT restate or replace the administrative clauses that governments publish for all contracts.
The foundation MUST NOT publish a full tender document.
Official model documents already cover the general terms, and their authors are writing new ones.
A pack that competed with them would leave buyers with two texts that conflict.

[[PRC-018]]
*PRC-018.* Each pack MUST include a table of the thresholds of the trade agreements.
The table MUST give them by kind of body and kind of contract.
The table MUST give its date, its source and the date when {abbr-fpsot} last checked it.
The drafting note MUST tell the buyer to check the values in force on the date of its tender.
The thresholds set which suppliers may bid, and governments change them every two years.

== Sources of the clause text

[[PRC-019]]
*PRC-019.* The text of a module MUST be original, or adapted from a source whose license allows commercial reuse.
Examples are the public domain, the Open Government `Licence` v3.0 of the United Kingdom and CC BY 4.0.
Each adapted passage MUST credit its source in the drafting note, as its license asks.
A module MUST NOT adapt a source under a noncommercial license or a ShareAlike license.
Such a source MAY appear in the drafting note as inspiration, with its title and link.
Firms that sell services will reuse the modules, and a ShareAlike term would spread to each tender.
