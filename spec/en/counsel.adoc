= Questions for counsel
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This page is informative.
It sets no rule, and it is not legal advice.
It lists the questions that a lawyer in public procurement must answer before any module leaves draft status, under PRC-012.
Each question gives what the research of the {abbr-fpsot-first} found, and what it could not settle.

== Complaints about open clauses

The {abbr-amp-first} has annulled Québec tenders that named one brand, or that only one supplier could meet.
A link:https://www.millerthomson.com/fr/perspectives-juridiques/bulletin-droit-de-la-construction-et-fr/appels-doffres-diriges-ou-en-sommes-nous-depuis-larrivee-de-lautorite-des-marches-publics/[law firm bulletin of 2023] sums up these decisions.
The research found no decision on a clause that requires an open standard or an open source license.
It found none on a clause that requires conformance to a specification from outside the formal standards bodies.

* Can a supplier complain to the {abbr-amp} that module M01 or M03 directs a tender, and on what grounds?
* Does the readiness rule of PRC-040 give a defense: one outside implementation, or two independent integrators?
* Is a license term, such as an approved open source license, a technical specification or a contract term?
* For a municipal body, the {abbr-amp} can only make recommendations. Does that change the risk?

== Article numbers after the reforms

The decrees of 2025 changed the {abbr-rcti-first}.
The {abbr-lcom-first} replaced the municipal rules on 2026-04-01.
The research could not read the official legal database of Québec.
It read other official copies, and found the numbers in this table.
A lawyer must verify each one.

* Are these numbers correct in the texts in force?
* Does the {abbr-rcti} let a body add the costs of software licenses and hosted services, or only of goods?
* When will a regulation let a municipal body adjust prices for additional costs?

== English license texts and French-only contracts

The Charter of the French language requires French for the contracts of the Québec administration and their related writings.
The ministry of the French language allows another language only in listed cases, says its link:https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/langue-francaise/fr/publications/emissaires/Guide_Contrats.pdf[guide on contracts].
Many open source licenses, such as the Apache License 2.0, exist only in English.
The LiLiQ licenses exist in French.
Creative Commons publishes CC BY 4.0 in an link:https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.fr[official French version].

* Can a Québec contract require a license whose only official text is in English?
* Does an official translation, such as the French version of CC BY 4.0, meet the rule?
* Must the contract say that the French version prevails? Can it say so for a license that another party wrote?

== Other questions

* *Contributors and conflicts of interest.* Some public bodies bar from a tender any party that helped prepare its documents. Does that rule reach a person who helped write a generic module that everyone can read? PRC-051 makes such work visible.
* *The federal policy on intellectual property.* Under supplemental conditions 4006, the contractor owns the foreground intellectual property. Does a duty to license it as open source need an exemption under the federal policy?
* *Equivalence under trade agreements.* Module M01 defines an equivalent by the same suite, or by an independent report. Does that definition meet the agreements that cover the tender, such as the {abbr-cfta-first}?
* *Credit under CC BY 4.0.* A buyer that adapts a module must credit the source. Does a credit line in a tender raise any issue?
* *The policy statement of 2026.* A Québec ministry published a statement on digital sovereignty and the purchase of technology. Has it become a binding policy?
* *Free software in the law.* The Québec framework quotes the duty to consider free software, with no article number. Which article holds that duty today?
