= Routes to market
include::_fpsot-terms.adoc[]

This page is informative.
It sets no rule, and it is not legal advice.
A tender helps one buyer at a time.
Some ways to buy let a single check of conformance serve many buyers, and this page describes them.
A lawyer must confirm each point before a buyer relies on it.

The article numbers on this page come from a link:https://www.publicationsduquebec.gouv.qc.ca/fileadmin/Fichiers_client/lois_et_reglements/CodificationsAdministratives/C65_1_R5_1.pdf[consolidated text] of the {abbr-rcti-first}.
Publications du Québec published it, current to 2026-10-02.
Under PRC-016, a lawyer must still verify each number.

== Homologation of goods

The {abbr-rcti} lets a public body run a {term-homologation} before a tender.
The body checks that a good conforms to a recognized standard or to an established technical specification.
A later tender may then admit only the goods on the list.
An {abbr-fpsot-first} conformance class, with its suite, fits the idea of an established technical specification.
The text that the foundation read puts these rules in articles 51 to 53.
It asks for a new notice at least once a year.

A body could homologate conforming products once, then buy among them on price, or on price and quality.
The government purchasing center could do the same for many bodies.

== Price request to qualified suppliers

Since the reform of 2025, a body may qualify suppliers first, then ask only them for a price.
The text that the foundation read places this route in articles 54 to 56.7 of the {abbr-rcti}.
A qualification can rest on conformance evidence, such as the statement and report of PRC-024.
The price request then stays short.

== Competitive dialogue

The {abbr-rcti} lets a body hold a competitive dialogue when its need is highly complex.
The head of the body must approve it.
The body refines a solution with each selected bidder, while an independent verifier watches the process.
Each bidder then makes a final offer.
The text that the foundation read places this route in article 19.
It suits a registry or another system that a body cannot fully describe in advance.

== The innovation space

The government procurement strategy of Québec for 2026 to 2030 plans tests of new ways to award contracts.
It names awards that look at the whole life cycle, and awards based on value.
It also asks bodies to reduce their dependence on exclusive rights.
Open specifications lower the costs of exit and migration, and a life-cycle award counts these costs.
The foundation could propose a pilot of its clause packs to this innovation space.

== Municipal bodies

Since 2026-04-01, municipal bodies buy under the {abbr-lcom-first}.
The link:https://cdn-contenu.quebec.ca/cdn-contenu/adm/min/affaires-municipales/publications/muni_express/GUI_muni_express_lcom.pdf[guide on the act] of the ministry of municipal affairs describes homologation and qualification.
It also says that a municipal body must describe its needs by performance and function, or else accept equivalents.
It places that duty in article 41 of the act, which a lawyer must verify.

== Ontario

The link:https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontarios-digital-and-data-directive-2021[digital and data directive] of Ontario requires its ministries to put its digital service standard into their procurement documents.
A later version of the packs could map each module to an item of that standard.
This version names the route only.
